APK source differences and the additional CPU prevent a causal claim that the handoff alone caused a measured delta.

## Fixed TCP download: 20 seconds, 4 × 16 sockets

The unprofiled, no-experiment-flags, 20-second ordinary VPN fixed-download suite ran A/B/B/A for N=2, then N=1 and N=3. Client CPU 4 and distinct source-port ranges are in each frozen manifest. `p99 upper` is the helper's probe upper bound, not a latency service-level claim. VPP CPU seconds are the sampled process-thread total over the sample coverage; CPU seconds/GiB extrapolates that rate to the client window and divides by endpoint-delivered bytes. CPU is neither whole-phone CPU nor energy.

For the matched N=2 A/B/B/A, old/new arithmetic mean endpoint throughput is **454.02/452.15 Mbps** (−0.41%); mean observed VPP CPU is **25.02/34.20 seconds** (+36.70%), and mean CPU seconds per delivered GiB is **24.16/33.41** (+38.32%). These two windows per artifact describe this load and placement; they do not establish a general throughput or latency change. N=1's 2163 credit rejects and two probe timeouts are explicit capacity/latency observations.

## Fixed UDP: same helper and 5-second windows

At an explicitly paced **8000 offered 1200-byte payload packets/s**, all arms use the same 25000–25067 source-port tuple range, client CPU 4 and A/B/B/A N=2 order. The packet source range is below the Pixel's observed ephemeral range `32768–60999`. Offered tickets, sent packets, pacing misses, peer receipts, duplicates and probe samples are retained separately. This is the suitable UDP cohort for comparing delivery at matched offered load; it is still only a five-second device observation.

N=2 old/new mean delivery is **76.72/76.52 Mbps** (−0.26%); mean observed VPP CPU is **4.25/5.79 seconds** (+36.40%), and mean CPU seconds/GiB **107.74/150.75** (+39.92%). The old runs lost no measured payload packets; the new runs lost 106 and 143, with almost matching primary-credit rejects. The p99 upper values also vary within the old A/A pair and one new run has a timeout, so they are per-window observations rather than a stable latency delta. N=1 and N=3 remain separate placement data.

The unpaced UDP cohort intentionally drove the same 4 × 16 sockets as fast as the helper could send. N=2 old/new mean endpoint delivery was **268.80/270.57 Mbps** while mean VPP CPU was **8.54/12.35 seconds**. Every N=2 window lost **1.17–1.22 million** payload packets and all 16 probes timed out; its 1–7 successful latency samples are **not latency evidence**. The old N=2 primary-credit rejects were 37940/28968, the new 172722/166908 with 25435/25135 matched TUN read pauses/resumes. The old artifact does not expose that read-pause counter, so its value is unknown. New N=1 delivered 115.25 Mbps with 334378 rejects; N=3 delivered 263.39 Mbps with 40879 rejects. This cohort exposes capacity behavior under overload; its near-equal N=2 delivered Mbps does not mean equal loss-free capacity. [All six raw stress windows](../../build-output/s304-vpn-udp-topology-20261022/campaign.json) remain separate from the paced comparison.

Across all 18 completed windows, Flow table capacity misses, return-credit/stamp/buffer failures, primary enqueue failures, and failed TUN writes were zero. The new artifact exposed TUN buffer allocation failure counts and all 12 new windows reported zero; the old artifact did not expose that counter. Per-window primary credit rejections, origin rejections, available read pauses and resumes are in the [machine-checked summary](../../build-output/s304-summary-20261022/summary.json), which uses `null` for old counters unavailable in its CLI. Every window retains native stop records with zero pending buffers, frames and obligations. A `COMPLETE` collector status means this integrity and cleanup protocol passed; it does not make a saturated or lossy run qualify for latency or adoption.

## Reproduction and provenance

The actual command entry points are [`pixel_quick.py`](../../tests/benchmark/pixel_quick.py), [`s304_vpn_topology.py`](../../tests/benchmark/s304_vpn_topology.py) and [`s304_summary.py`](../../tests/benchmark/s304_summary.py). Run from the repo root with Pixel stopped, ordinary VPN consent and KernelSU grant intact, saved Openwrt LAN selected, and `build-output/s304-prepared/socket-load-host server 192.168.4.5 41723` serving on the Host. Use the repo-local `./dev/adb` wrapper, and restore the previous APK/Wi-Fi afterward. Each frozen manifest holds workload, helper and collector hashes; prepare a new manifest when the collector source changes. `s304_vpn_topology.py` installs old/new APKs in its fixed sequence, records installed native hashes, runs each collector and restores the specified original APK in `finally`.
